Fifteen emails. Nobody received all of them.

An exhibitor nurture for WHX Dubai, built in Oracle Eloqua, where a lead score decided who moved forward, who got a nudge and who stopped hearing from us the moment they booked.

My role

As Senior Commercial Marketing Executive on WHX Dubai, I designed the nurture, wrote the fifteen emails, built the flows in Oracle Eloqua and maintained the lead-scoring scale that decided each contact's path.

I also ran the sponsorship upsell at the end of the journey, which sat with marketing, not sales.

    The rules underneath

    Behaviour decided the next email

    01 · Hooks

    A different reason every time

    Each email ran on its own hook, from agenda pages and keynote speakers to government involvement, the brochure and the floorplan.

    02 · Waits

    One to two days, then the next

    If a contact did not interact, the next email in the stage followed after a one to two day wait.

    03 · Score

    A lead score from 0 to 100

    Interaction raised the score and moved the contact on to the next stage.

    04 · Stalled intent

    Urgency only where it was earned

    High-intent contacts who stalled got a branch built mainly on competitor momentum, with stands running out and health-sector news.

    05 · Suppression

    Booked means stop

    Once an exhibitor booked, they were suppressed from the booking emails straight away.

    06 · Upsell

    Sponsorship after the stand

    Exhibitors who had booked moved to a sponsorship upsell, which I ran from marketing.

    What the flows held

    0-100lead score, maintained by meEvery interaction moved the score, and the score, not a calendar, triggered the next email.

    Alongside the journey I ran A/B tests on subject lines and content variants to find the higher-performing segments.

    The view behind it

    Most nurture sequences are built around the sender's calendar, not the reader's behaviour.

    The industry default is a fixed drip: everyone gets every email, on a timer. I would rather write more and send less. The right number of emails is not a number. It is whatever each reader's behaviour earns.
